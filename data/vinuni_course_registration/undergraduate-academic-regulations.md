# Academic Regulations for Full-Time Undergraduate Programs

This excerpt contains only sections needed for course registration, study load, add/drop, withdrawal, prerequisites, and related exceptions.

## Article 10. Course Registration

At the beginning of each academic year, the Office of Registrar announces the tentative academic calendar of each program, the list of required and elective courses, course descriptions, prerequisites for registration, exam periods, and formats of exams and assessment.

Students must register for courses with the Office of Registrar. Registration must be based on academic capability, course prerequisites, and program requirements.

Students are responsible for their studies at the University. Students must understand curriculum requirements and plan ahead to complete the program within the permitted duration.

Student registration is accepted by the Office of Registrar only if it complies with current University regulations. Exceptions must be approved by the Program Director.

## Article 11. Study Load

Study load is determined by the credits the student is studying. A normal full-time load is 30 credits spread over an academic year.

To be classified as a full-time student, a student must be enrolled in at least 80% of a normal full-time load in a regular semester, equivalent to 12 credits.

Normal load in a regular semester is at least 14 credits for first-semester students and students with good academic standing or higher in the previous semester, except the final semester. For students with poor academic standing in the previous semester, normal load is at least 10 credits and no more than 14 credits.

Automatic overload is 18 to 22 credits, subject to review by the Academic Advisor or Program Director.

Beyond automatic overload is more than 22 credits and is subject to approval by the College Dean or Dean-designee.

Summer session allows up to 9 credits with no required minimum study load. Summer workload is calculated only for the summer semester as a separate or supplementary semester, not for regular Fall or Spring semesters.

Students must petition for overloading if they are first-semester first-year students requesting more than 18 credits, are not in good academic standing in the previous semester and wish to take more than 18 credits, or request more than 22 credits in a regular semester.

The total number of credits, including physical education, support, repeated courses, and non-credit courses, is used to calculate overloading.

Prerequisites: students registering for a course must meet prerequisite requirements of each course and academic sequences of a particular program. Exemptions from prerequisites require Program Director approval.

## Article 12. Course Add, Drop, and Withdrawal

Students may add a course to their schedule no later than the close of the 10th business day of the regular semester and no later than the close of the 5th business day of mini semesters such as Fall A/B or Spring A/B.

Students may drop a class within the drop deadline published in the academic calendar for that semester. The drop deadline for a regular semester is no later than the close of the 15th business day of the semester and no later than the close of the 10th business day of Summer. Changes made in this period are not included in the academic record.

Dropping a class after the add/drop period has ended is considered a Withdrawal. Students may withdraw from a course after the add/drop period with no grade penalty. Students receive a "Withdrawal - W" grade on the transcript for the course when they withdraw.

Students who wish to add a course after the add/drop period has ended need to petition with approval from their academic advisor or Office of Registrar. The instructor may allow the addition at her or his discretion.

## Course Withdrawal Policy

A "W-Withdrawn" grade is assigned on the transcript when a student has registered for the course in a semester and later submitted a notification of withdrawal to the University. The W indicates that students attempted the class but withdrew before completing it for a letter grade.

Withdrawal from a course is permitted after the course dropping period and must occur before completing no more than 30% of the study time for that course. After that date, withdrawals are no longer permitted, and students are graded according to course performance. If the student withdraws before the Drop Date for the semester, no W grade is recorded on the transcript.

The withdrawal limit policy allows students to withdraw from a maximum of 18 credits over the entire program of study. Once the limit is reached, students can no longer withdraw and must remain enrolled. Instructors assign appropriate grades.
